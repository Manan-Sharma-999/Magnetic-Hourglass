Field source

0°: solenoid around the hourglass. 90°: Helmholtz pair with a horizontal axis.

B = G(z−z0) along the axis, −G r/2 across it. A uniform bias field along the axis only moves the zero.

B = μ0I / 2πr. A vertical wire gives a horizontal field at the neck, a horizontal wire a vertical one.

Small NdFeB magnet on the axis, magnetised vertically (point dipole).

Model switches and contact parameters

What is simulated. Soft-sphere grains (linear spring–dashpot contacts with friction, rotation and rolling resistance) in a conical neck under constant head; grains that leave are put back on the free surface. Each filing carries a moment induced by the applied field plus the fields of filings within the cutoff, with the near-contact force enhancement of Sikka et al. and a local-field cap of 3B. Beads are non-magnetic and 24% lighter. The field of the whole magnetised charge, included in our full simulations, is left out here for speed.

Units. Lengths scale with d, times with τ = √(d/g) (6.8 ms for 0.45 mm). The magnetic Bond number Bo = sF(B/B1)² with B1 = 4.4 mT·(d/0.45 mm)1/2 compares the contact attraction of two touching spheres along the field (18.8 Bo mg) with a grain’s weight.

Model. (Q/Q0)² = 1 + Γm − (Bom/Bo*)ν with Bom = φβ(ρF/ρ̄)Bo, using constants fitted to our full simulations of this hourglass. The cohesion is taken from the field about one neck diameter above the outlet (the probe height zp), where the grains break their bonds; the Kelvin pull Γ from the gradient in the arch region. Time-dependent drives are averaged over a response window Tw of about 3τ (20 ms).

The simulation runs entirely in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere. Speed depends on your computer; a desktop browser is best.
